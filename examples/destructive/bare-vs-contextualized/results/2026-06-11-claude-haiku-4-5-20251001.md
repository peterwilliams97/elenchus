# Calibration — bare vs contextualized

- date: 2026-06-11
- model: `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`
- runs: 10 each input
- mode: substance

## Verdict distributions

| Input | claims/run | hollow | partial | substantive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bare.txt` | 1 | 10 | 0 | 0 |
| `contextual.txt` | 6 | 28 | 21 | 11 |

At the **aggregate** level the distribution shifts toward `substantive`/`partial` under context
(0% → 53%). But that is a **decomposition artifact** — `contextual.txt` decomposes into 6 fragments,
and the shift is carried entirely by the *added* sub-claims.

## Central-claim attribution (the unit-of-analysis check)

The claim under test is identical in both inputs. Its verdict is **not**:

| Fragment | verdict across 10 contextual runs |
|---|---|
| **CENTRAL: "iPhone won't get significant market share"** | **`hollow` 10/10** |
| sub: $500 subsidized | hollow 6 · partial 3 · substantive 1 |
| sub: 1.3B phones/yr | substantive 6 · partial 3 · hollow 1 |
| sub: Apple ~2–3% | partial 9 · hollow 8 · substantive 3 |
| sub: licensed platform 60–80% | partial 6 · hollow 3 · substantive 1 |

**The central prediction rates `hollow` 10/10 with *and* without context.** The substantive/partial
verdicts all land on the concrete added sub-claims (market size, share figures), not the claim the
probe is about.

## Killing axis (mechanism)

| | bare central claim | contextual central claim |
|---|---|---|
| **fatal** | **Counterexample 9/10** | **Counterexample 8/10** |
| weakens | Evidence 9/10, Causality 10/10, others | Evidence 7/10, Base-rate 8/10, others |

The fatal blow is **Counterexample**, not Evidence. A sweeping forward prediction ("X will not get
significant share") always admits a constructible counterexample, and the critic treats that as fatal
— independent of whether the claim's own argument is present in the text. Evidence only ever
*weakens*; it is not the mechanism.
