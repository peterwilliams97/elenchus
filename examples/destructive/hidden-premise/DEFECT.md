# DEFECT — hidden-premise chain (target axis: **Hidden premise**)

> Openly-constructed adversarial probe. The construction *is* the ground truth. See `../README.md`.

## The engineered defect

A conclusion that is valid **only under an unstated, load-bearing premise**. The argument is:

> 91% of *new signups* chose cloud → therefore retire on-prem and put engineering effort there.

This follows only if you assume the hidden premise: **the new-signup mix is representative of where
revenue and strategic value sit.** That premise is both unstated and dubious — the on-prem base is
plausibly the legacy, high-revenue enterprise accounts who *never appear among new signups at all*.
The 91% figure is about the wrong population for the decision it is asked to support.

This targets the Hidden-premise axis directly: *"what unstated assumption must hold?"* — here, that
new-customer acquisition mix proxies installed-base value.

## Axes it is deliberately clean on

- **Evidence** — a real, specific figure (91%) is given; not bare assertion.
- **Base rate / magnitude** — a genuine quantity *and* population are present; the number itself is
  not the flaw (the *inference from* it is). The base-rate axis should find the quantity fine.
- **Falsifiability** — the signup figure is checkable; the claim is not vacuous.
- **Equivocation** — terms are stable.

## Most likely *adjacent* catch

- **Counterexample** — "the on-prem customers might be your biggest accounts" is a counterexample to
  the representativeness premise. A Counterexample finding is the same defect under another axis name.

## How this probe was constructed

Built a clean syllogism with one missing middle premise doing all the load-bearing work, chose a
figure (91%) large enough to feel decisive so the *number* is not the temptation to attack — forcing
any real critique onto the **inference**. No equivocation, no causal overreach, no magnitude gap was
added, isolating the hidden-premise signal.
